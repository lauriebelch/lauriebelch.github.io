Laurence Belcher

Department of Biology, University of Oxford

My research is on the genomics of adaptation. I want to know how change in a genome produces the traits an organism needs, and I go after that question with experiments, population genetics and comparisons across thousands of species at once.

The longest-running thread is the evolution of social behaviour. Why do bacteria help each other when cheating is an option, and what does a history of helping leave behind in a genome? I have shown that cooperation in wild bacterial populations is favoured by kin selection, and that development itself can hold cheating in check in a social amoeba. Alongside that I work on what a species' way of life does to its gene content, and on what patterns of gene gain and loss reveal about which genes matter.

All of this depends on the methods being good enough, so I build them as well. I lead the development of OrthoFinder and the team behind it. I wrote GLADE, SOCfinder and OrthoSimulator.

I have taught biology at St John's College since 2021. I give small-group tutorials across evolutionary biology, ecology and statistics, to undergraduates in every year group. I have also lectured on the department's undergraduate course, and I co-supervise PhD students.

  • OrthoFinder
    Phylogenetic orthology inference. Cited by more than ten thousand studies. Development lead.
  • GLADE
    Infers gene gains, losses, duplications and ancestral gene content across a species tree. Sole developer.
  • SOCfinder
    Finds the genes behind cooperative behaviour in bacteria. Lead developer.
  • OrthoSimulator
    Simulated genomes with exact evolutionary ground truth, for benchmarking orthology methods. Co-developer.
  • COPSE
    Detects gene families that evolve together, from shared losses and duplications. Lead developer. In preparation.
